## 3. Research output (engine forensics, not paper study)

Two findings with takeaways for the fusion-mlx team (full log:
`docs/handoff-fusion-mlx.md` in code-research):

1. **Degradation slip anatomy (fusion-mlx#1694).** Under concurrent Kimi-48B
   tool-call traffic, a Kimi parse failure coincides (0 ms - 90 s) with a
   27B request emitting its tool call as plain text `call_<hex>{...}` —
   arguments-only JSON, no tool-name key, coherent own-task content. Slip
   ids are 8-hex (minted by the parse path; request ids are 12-hex).
   Epoch invalidation (b6edee57) reduced but did not eliminate (2 slips in
   the post-fix run, now simultaneous with the Kimi parse failure instead
   of lagging). Takeaway for harness design: text-recovery must handle
   name-less emissions, not just wrong-wrapper ones.
2. **T05 latency root cause: episodic throughput decay.** Engine logs show
   27B single-stream completion rate oscillating between ~17 tok/s baseline
   and 6-8 tok/s episodes; after a 3-min idle gap a burst hits 30 tok/s and
   decays to 6 over ~4 min of sustained decoding, recovering after pauses.
   Kimi traffic had ended before the slow window — signature matches
   thermal/GPU-clock throttling, not concurrency. T05 failed solely by
   landing its two long completions mid-decay (~460 s generation vs 600 s
   cap). No harness-side mitigation; ask logged with fusion-mlx team.
